
52. **A notion is never said before the one it depends on is understood — and the course starts with the right door** (user rule, 26 November 2026 — « auditer les cours pour ne pas perturber l'élève : quelle notion il doit commencer, et quelle notion ne doit pas être dite maintenant jusqu'à ce qu'il comprenne la notion d'avant »; the length of the courses is kept). Before writing a course, its notions are listed with what each one needs (the dependency list goes into `backend/data/curriculum/architecture/<deck>.md`); the course follows that order. « Understood » means: explained, shown on an example, and, at the end of the block, checked — not just named. One new notion per slide; a slide that needs a notion placed later is moved after it; a word of the chapter (or of an earlier year) never appears in a voice, a card, a question or an option before the slide that teaches it (rule 27, rule 34). The first notion taught is the entry door the others hang on (the concrete phenomenon or object, never a formula or a definition with words still unknown). Audit: `docs/audit-pedagogie/ordre-des-notions/<deck>.md` (read slide by slide, one report per course).

53. **No question in the first slides** (user rule, 26 November 2026 — « ne pas poser les questions aux premières diapos »). The opening and the first explanations let the student settle in: **the first 6 slides of a course carry no question** — no `question`, no `exercises`, no `observation`, and no voice that ends on « ? » (a rhetorical question stays inside the sentence and the tutor answers it himself). The first question is the one of the first block that taught something new, asked after that block's last slide (rule 27). Checked by `scripts/audit/audit_pedagogie.py` (column « Questions dans les 6 premières » = 0).

54. **What is said is written at that word — not before, not at the end** (user rule, 26 November 2026, rules 18 and 39 made precise for the board). Every formula, relation, number with its unit or definition the voice says is written (by `concentration.centre`, or by a `play_until` of the simulation that writes it) **at the word that says it**: not already on a card when the slide opens, not only at the « Je relis » at the end. A slide of conversions or of several formulas writes them one by one, each at its sentence (`op_t5_convertir` said four conversions with no gesture; `lc_rp2_bobine` wrote u_b = L·di/dt + r·i only at the re-reading). A sentence of more than ~60 words without any gesture is a defect (the eye has nothing to follow). Checked by `audit_pedagogie.py` (« Formules dites non écrites », « Passages ≥ 60 mots sans geste »), then by eye on the flagged slides.

55. **An important notion is announced, said, said again another way, and recalled later — and the announcement is never the same sentence** (user rule, 26 November 2026 — « les notions importantes doivent être dites et redites ; avant d'évoquer une notion importante, il demande à l'élève de suivre attentivement cette partie, et à chaque fois il le dit d'une autre manière »). Important = a definition of the programme, a formula, a method expected at the bac, a classic trap. For each:
   - **Announce it** in one short sentence just before, asking for attention — a different sentence each time in the course (bank: `docs/audit-pedagogie/banque-de-phrases.md`). This is not « رد البال » used for a trap after the fact: the announcement comes *before* the notion and asks the student to follow.
   - **Say it, written at the same word** (rule 54), then **say it again another way** on the same slide (rule 34: an image, an example, the same idea from another view), then **re-read it word for word** (rule 5).
   - **Recall it later**: at least once in a later slide that uses it (« تفكر… », « شفنا قبل… »), in other words, before the end-of-chapter summary.
   Measured by `audit_pedagogie.py` (« Notions clés » / « redites » / « annoncées (phrases ≠) ») and listed per course in `ordre-des-notions/<deck>.md` (« Notions importantes dites une seule fois »).

56. **A human teacher, not a robot** (user rule, 26 November 2026 — « humaniser notre tuteur, ne pas le rendre robotique »). The student must feel someone who talks *to him*:
   - **Varied openings, real transitions.** Never two slides in a row that start the same way, and no opening word on more than ~10 % of a course's slides (measured on 26/11/2026: 413 of 1 696 slides start with « دابا », 18 slides of RLC start with « نفس التمرين ديال الامتحان الوطني »). A slide starts by linking to what was just seen (« شفنا أن… ، دابا… ») or by what the student sees, not by a formula of the template.
   - **Reactions that answer this student.** A reaction names what was right in *this* choice and the precise idea behind the mistake; its first words vary (measured: 303 wrong-answer reactions start with « شوف، الجواب », 155 right-answer ones with « مزيان، الجواب »); praise names what was well done (rule 9).
   - **Human touches, measured**: surprise in front of a result (« شوف هاد الحاجة، غريبة شوية! »), sharing the difficulty (« هادي كيغلطو فيها بزاف ديال التلاميذ، عادي »), encouragement tied to effort, the reason a notion matters, a short image from daily life — one or two per block, never a joke on the student, never filler.
   - **Talk, don't recite**: short sentences, « tu », questions the tutor answers himself inside the explanation, a pause after an important result (`duree_s` / silence) instead of chaining.
   - Same rule for the live tutor (`llm_service.py` prompt) and the reprise cycle. Checked by `audit_pedagogie.py` (« Débuts de voix répétés », « Tournures recopiées ≥ 5 diapos », reactions with the same first words).
