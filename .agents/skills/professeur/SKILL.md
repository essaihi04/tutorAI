# Professeur

The tutor teaches like an experienced teacher: it decides at every turn what the teaching move is, and the animated professor embodies it on screen.

## The rules (from a teacher with ten years of practice, October 2026)

1. **Explain in one complete block, then stop.** An explanation is 5 to 8 sentences without interruption: the idea, a concrete example, the formula and the meaning of each term. Then exactly **one** question. Never a monologue beyond that, never two questions.
2. **Check understanding by reformulation.** After a block, the student explains it in their own words, "as to a classmate". Only then a short application question.
3. **Error: hint, second try, then the answer.** Name what is right, give one targeted hint, let the student try again. The answer is given only after two failed attempts, with its explanation, followed by a twin question (same idea, other example) to check.
4. **Unclear answer: ask once, then judge.** An answer that cannot be judged (ambiguous, cut off, badly transcribed) is neither right nor wrong: the tutor asks the student to clarify, with no hint and no attempt counted. Only once; on the next turn it must judge.
5. **Re-read what must be learnt word for word.** At the end of an explanation block, the tutor re-reads the one key sentence (definition, or formula with the meaning of each term), announced by "Je relis :", exactly as on the board. When the student asks to hear it again ("répète", "عاود", "ما سمعتش"), the tutor re-reads it identically and adds nothing; this is not an attempt. "Je ne comprends pas" is different: change the angle. "se répète" is a physics answer, not a request.
6. **Read a problem statement twice before any calculation.** First reading: "what is this exercise about?" in one sentence. Second, active reading: the data with their units, what is asked, and the verb of the instruction (calculer, justifier, montrer que, déduire). Neither reading is graded (`<eval>aucune</eval>`); solving starts after.
7. **Prerequisite gap: open a parenthesis, then come back.** When the error comes from a competency of earlier years (TC, 1re bac, collège — e.g. derivatives, projecting a vector, cell structure), the tutor says "On ouvre une parenthèse : [titre]", re-explains it in 3 to 5 sentences with an example from the original level, re-reads the key sentence, asks one verification question, then says "On ferme la parenthèse" and re-asks the course question unchanged, with fresh attempts. Never nested, at most 3 per session. The candidate prerequisites per chapter live in `backend/data/curriculum/prerequis.json` (each with its official source) and are offered to the model only after a first failed attempt; the model declares one with `<prerequis>id</prerequis>`, ids outside the chapter's list are ignored. Opening/closing is sent to the screen (`parenthese` event) and the closing is recorded in `student_answer_history` with `source = "parenthese"`.
8. **Stay inside the programme's perimeter.** For chapters that have a sheet in `backend/data/curriculum/perimetre.json` (first one: dipôle RC), the prompt receives the chapter's notions, the methods expected at the bac, what is admitted without proof, and the terms not to use with what to say instead (no Laplace, no complex impedance, no Bode for the RC…). After each reply, `perimetre.detecter` re-reads the displayed text; in this first phase it only logs to `data/perimetre_journal.jsonl` (count with `scripts/decompte_perimetre.py`), it never corrects. Enforcement comes only once the lists have been checked against real replies. For the COURSES, the same perimeter is enforced before publication by `controler_cadre.py` against `backend/data/curriculum/cadre_reference_2bac_pc.json` (capacities of the official cadre de référence, formulas as written and said, out-of-scope words) — skill `cours-concentration`, step 8.
9. **Warm and demanding.** Praise precisely what is earned and why; name what is missing without detours; never move on while the student is approximate. "Almost" only when it is true.
10. **Write as the marking scheme reads** (learnt from real Moroccan teachers, November 2026, like rules 11–15). Every calculation: the relation with the statement's own symbols, the numerical substitution, the result, the unit — each one earns marks; a single numerical substitution at the end; answer the question asked and nothing more. A calculated answer missing the relation, the substitution or the unit is at best `partiel`.
11. **Check before validating.** Compare a numerical result with the chapter's reference order of magnitude, and redo it by a second route when one exists. An absurd result is pointed out, never written down.
12. **Read the axis before the graph.** In front of a curve, an oscillogram or a photo: what is on the horizontal axis, in which unit, what one division is worth — before reading any value.
13. **Recognise the standard case.** Spot the statement's code word ("réfléchi", "deux milieux", "t₁ inconnu"…), name the case, draw the situation, apply the case's method, then vary one parameter ("and if the receiver moves away?").
14. **A definition is built by three questions** — what? where? which characteristics? — then re-read word for word (rule 5).
15. **A multiple-choice item is read to its last word** and justified by the word that decides it.
16. **Chapter experience, served when it helps.** `backend/data/curriculum/experience_profs.json` holds, per chapter (keys of `perimetre.json`), what teachers repeat, the frequent mistakes with the correcting move, the standard cases with their method, reference values, approximations teachers say that must NOT be repeated, and what was excluded as out of programme. `experience_profs.py` serves it in pieces: the insistences and "ne dis pas" when explaining, the standard cases and reference values when solving starts, the mistakes only after a first failed attempt. Standard cases are all named, but only those whose clues appear in the student's text get their full method (keeps the block short). `questions_eleves` holds questions real students ask under the teachers' videos, with the correct answer; `bloc_questions` serves the two closest to the student's message, so a free question is answered the way it should be. After each reply `experience_profs.detecter` logs repeated approximations to `data/experience_journal.jsonl` (observation only). Every entry must be checked against `perimetre.json`; `test_experience_profs.py` fails if an out-of-programme term is taught. First chapter: `ondes_mecaniques`.

17. **The spoken darija script is simple and uses only what the voice was trained on** (user rule, 7 November 2026). The full procedure — French for equations, operations, numbers, units, « en fonction de » and exam words; checking, generating and verifying the voice — is the `voix-du-cours` skill. Applies to every `speech_text.mixed` of a course and to anything the voice reads aloud. That includes cutting every all-French sentence longer than ten words with commas: in one long piece the voice loses its way and mumbles. This holds in the course and in the live replies to free questions. The voice model (v4, `ferdaous04/academy-v4-entrainement`) learnt from 38 852 sentences of Moroccan teachers (`zouhairessaihi/academy-v4-selection`, `selection_train.jsonl`, 28 % of the words French): a word it never heard is mispronounced, and a word the students do not know is not understood either.
    - **French only for three things:** the programme's technical terms (l'onde, la perturbation, la célérité, le retard, le milieu, le front, l'élongation, l'aspect, la source, la propagation, transversale, longitudinale, la sensibilité, division, l'oscilloscope, le récepteur…), the **numbers** said the way BIOF teachers say them in class (deux, zéro virgule deux, trois cent quarante, mille cinq cents — the corpus has deux 4 675 times, zéro 3 549, virgule 418), and the simple French words every student already uses (donc, alors, voilà, d'accord, c'est, bien, déjà, normalement).
    - **Any other French word goes into simple darija.** Not in the corpus and not known by most students, so neither the voice nor the student gets it: « la bouée » (0 times; heard back as « la boue »), « la queue » (0), « la manip » (0), « clap » (0), « impulsion » (0), « le barème » (0), « l'énoncé » (0), « trajet » (0), « divisée » (0), « oscillogramme » (0), « l'échographie » (0). Say instead, with words the corpus does contain: « واحد الكورة صغيرة فوق الما » (bouée), « اللخر ديال la perturbation » (queue), « فالتجربة » (manip), « كنضرب بيدي » / « le signal » (clap, impulsion), « النقط » (barème), « السؤال » / « التمرين » (énoncé), « المسافة » (trajet), « مقسومة على » (divisée par).
    - **Symbols as the corpus writes them:** « teta » (335 times; « thêta » 0), « tau » (426), « y grec ديال M », « delta t », « lambda ».
    - **Darija from the corpus, short sentences.** Prefer the words teachers use most: يعني، دابا، إذن، هادي/هذا، شنو، كيفاش، علاش، واش، باش، شحال، مثلا، مزيان، بحال، ملي، **يلا** (3 505 times, rather than « إلا », 492), كيساوي/كتساوي. Avoid rare, literary or long glued forms (« فالمراية », « مزحزحين », « كتحسم », « وكيبعدو » never appear): one idea per sentence, the verb near the start, no stacked « و…ف… » prefixes.
    - **Never add tashkil to fix a word.** The corpus has none; harakat push the voice off its training and make it worse. A darija word that seems to need vowels is replaced by a simpler, more common one (or said in French if it is a programme term). The live tutor follows the same rule (`llm_service.py`, [RÈGLE DE SÉCURITÉ — PRONONCIATION ET FRANÇAIS]).
    - **Check before generating.** Every French word of the script and every rare darija word is looked up in `selection_train.jsonl`. A French word seen fewer than 20 times that is not a programme term is replaced. A programme term rarely seen (« l'élongation » 62, « réfléchie » 1, « l'écho » 0) stays, because the student must hear it for the bac, but it is said with its article and once explained in darija (« réfléchie، يعني كترجع »). The full count for the waves example is in the shared notes (`voix-dataset-v4.md`).

18. **Whatever the tutor says about a simulation, the simulation shows it at that moment** (user rule, 8 November 2026). A phase that is described (« M monte, descend, revient à sa place », « voilà le retard τ », « toute la perturbation est sur la corde ») is not described over a scene that already finished: the tutor replays the simulation and freezes it on that very instant (`play_until {moment}`), then points at the landmark (`highlight`). Several phases said in a row are shown one after the other (monte → freeze, descend → freeze, revient → freeze); a quantity being defined is shown frozen where it is largest or readable (l'élongation: M at its top, with the y arrow). So the key instants are **extracted from the simulation first**: each simulation exposes named `moments` computed from its physics (`simulations-au-tableau` skill), and the course script (`concentration.pointage` of a slide, `{mot, commande: "play_until", parametres: {moment}}`) or the live tutor targets them by name, never by guessed seconds. In the concentration player (`CourseFocusStage.tsx`) a command cue leaves 0,9 s before its word, the time for the professor's hand to reach the button; measured on the rope slide, each freeze lands within half a second of its word.

19. **A variant is shown by the tutor, and the student's own result is interpreted on the spot** (user rule, 8 November 2026). If a simulation can be varied, the tutor varies it himself after explaining (M₂ twice as far, the bottom lower) to give the comparison. When the student varies it himself, he gets an answer about HIS result: what changed, with the values, and why (the law of the course); then one question on that result: vrai/faux if he just made a mistake, QCM by default, a direct question once he answers right. It is generated at that moment (DeepSeek, `app/services/simulation_reaction.py`, endpoint `/course-player/sim-reaction`), said at once by the live voice under rule 17, and the student stays on the same slide. Test: `app/tests/test_simulation_reaction.py`.

20. **Questions are shown at once and every answer gets the tutor's voice** (user rule, 8 November 2026). In the concentration player the slide's question and exercises open as soon as the voice ends (« ✏️ À toi », no hunting for a button). Each answer — a QCM choice, each vrai/faux statement, a written answer — gets a reaction generated at that moment (DeepSeek, `app/services/reponse_eleve.py`, endpoint `/course-player/answer-reaction`, about 2 s) and said by the live voice: right → what is right and why; wrong at the first try → what is right in it, one targeted hint, no answer, the wrong choice is struck out and he tries again (rule 3); wrong again → the answer and its justification. When the exercise carries its key, the verdict is imposed on the model, which only explains it. Test: `app/tests/test_reponse_eleve.py`. **Every possible answer is prepared in advance** (user rule, 8 November 2026): each question of a course carries its key (`question.correct`, options shuffled so the right one is not always first), and for EVERY option the tutor's reaction is written beforehand with the same prompt and stored in the slide — `concentration.reponses[cle][option]`: `juste` for the right option, `1` (hint) and `2` (answer) for each wrong one — with its voice track generated by the course voice (`/media/audio/courses/<deck>/reponses/`). The player plays the prepared reaction at once, with no server; only a free written answer still goes to DeepSeek and the live voice. A new course is not finished until all its questions have their prepared reactions and tracks. **The body answers too** (user rule, 8 November 2026): with the voice, the 3D tutor expresses the reaction — right at the first try → `celebrate` (arms up, sparks), right after the hint → `encourage`, wrong at the first try → `wrong` (shakes his head, 💡), wrong again → `encourage`, an answer he cannot judge → `think`, a result the student got by himself in the simulation → `surprise` (`AIAvatar emotion`, `CourseFocusStage.ressentir`).

21. **Name the view before using it** (user rule, 8 November 2026). Students do not know what a « vue de dessus », a « vue de côté », a « profil », a « coupe » or a graph « y en fonction de x » shows, nor what « transversale / longitudinale » means in the picture. Before any figure or simulation with several views, the tutor says, in one sentence each, what each view is and where the eye is (« la vue de dessus : كنشوفو الما من الفوق » ; « la vue de côté : من الجنب، باش نشوفو الكورة كتطلع وكتهبط »), pointing at it; before « transversale » or « longitudinale » he shows WHERE the point moves and WHERE the wave goes, then names it. This holds in the course scripts (the slide's spoken text starts with the views, with a `pointage` on each) and in the live replies (the simulation's `ce_qui_se_voit` names its views; the tutor repeats them when he points there). Example: `wx_o1_bouee`.

22. **The reprise cycle: nobody moves on before it is understood, and nothing is explained twice the same way** (user rule, 8 November 2026). In the concentration player, when the slide's questions are all right, the board closes after the bravo and the course goes to the next slide by itself. When one is wrong (after the hint and the second try), the tutor stays on the SAME slide and re-explains ANOTHER way — with the full context: what he already said, the question, the wrong answers, the methods already tried, the methods that work for this student (skill `suivi-des-acquis`), the simulation's instants and landmarks — while piloting the figure (`play_until`, `highlight`); then he asks a TWIN question (same competence, other form or values, one try). Right → next slide. Wrong → next cycle with another entry: 1 = the simulation frozen step by step, 2 = a worked example with other numbers, 3 = the answer itself, justified, written in the notebook. **Three cycles at most**; after the third the competence is marked NOT ACQUIRED with the methods tried, and the student moves on. The student may leave the slide by hand at any cycle: the competence is then marked NOT ACQUIRED too (`cycle<n>_abandon`), so the free mode and the next course take it back. Code: `app/services/reexplication.py`, endpoint `/course-player/reexplication`, `CourseFocusStage.lancerCycle`; test `app/tests/test_reexplication.py`.

23. **The student can raise a hand at any moment — in writing, by voice or with an image** (user rule, 8 November 2026). The « 🙋 Question » button of the concentration player pauses the course and the simulation, and takes a written question, a microphone recording (transcribed in darija by the STT, French technical terms kept) or an image (a photo of an exercise, a diagram, a notebook page: text read by the OCR). The tutor answers WITHOUT leaving the slide, with the whole context — what he just said, what the simulation shows (`ce_qui_se_voit`), its instants and landmarks (he points while answering) — in 3 to 6 sentences, then brings the student back to the course; a question from another chapter gets two sentences and « ça viendra » ; out of the programme → said so (rule 8); an exercise on an image → the case, the relation and the first step, never the final answer. The question is recorded in the acquis as `question_libre` (not judged), so the free mode knows what the student asked. Code: `app/services/question_eleve.py`, endpoint `/course-player/question-eleve`, `CourseFocusStage.leverLaMain`; test `app/tests/test_question_eleve.py`.

## How it is implemented

- `backend/app/services/skill_professeur.py` — `SkillProfesseur` keeps three facts per session (a question is pending, number of attempts, turns) and returns the turn's instruction block `[GESTE DE PROF — ce tour]`, injected into the system prompt before the model speaks. The model declares its judgement of the student's answer at the start of its reply in `<eval>correct|partiel|faux|incertain|aucune</eval>`; the module reads it to count attempts (`incertain`: the answer cannot be judged, so the tutor asks once for clarification without counting an attempt), and `tag_decoder` strips the tag from chat, voice and history.
- Lesson phases (`lesson_phase.py`, `PHASE_RULES` in `llm_service.py`) say what the phase is for; the skill says what to do in this turn.
- Surfaces (scene, image, 3D, simulation, notebook) are chosen by `routeur_pedagogique.py`; explanation tactics per student by `teaching_tactics.py`; tone by `humanisation.py`.
- The animated professor (`frontend/src/services/professorEmotion.js`, `AIAvatar.tsx`, `Professor3D.tsx`) reacts to the tutor's words: greets, celebrates earned success, puts hands on heart, nods to encourage, laughs, is surprised; it points, writes on the board and operates simulations itself. The declared `<eval>` also reaches it directly (`professor_reaction`: correct → arms up, faux → shakes his head, partiel → nod), as does a right or wrong check inside a lab.
- Simulations drawn on the board (`docs/design/labos-au-tableau.md`) announce pointable landmarks and an explanation script; `backend/app/services/simulation_explication.py` puts them in the prompt (`bloc_prompt`): the tutor explains landmark by landmark with `highlight {target}` (the professor flies into the simulation and points), relaunches the simulation itself when the student does not understand, and after a failed check the server gives one hint pointing at the missing landmark, then relaunches and re-explains differently (`decider_reaction`, `_sim_reagir_verification`).

## When changing the teaching behaviour

- Change the rule in `skill_professeur.py` and its test `app/tests/test_skill_professeur.py` together; keep instructions imperative and short — a long instruction gets paraphrased instead of followed.
- Never make the model's judgement implicit: if a new state depends on what the student did, it must be declared in the reply (a tag) or measured from the conversation, never guessed.
- Run `python -m pytest app/tests/test_skill_professeur.py app/tests/test_experience_profs.py app/tests/test_tag_decoder.py app/tests/test_lesson_phase.py app/tests/test_simulation_explication.py` from `backend`.
