# Professeur

The tutor teaches like an experienced teacher: it decides at every turn what the teaching move is, and the animated professor embodies it on screen.

## The rules (from a teacher with ten years of practice, October 2026)

1. **Explain in one complete block, then stop.** An explanation is 5 to 8 sentences without interruption: the idea, a concrete example, the formula and the meaning of each term. Then exactly **one** question. Never a monologue beyond that, never two questions.
2. **Check understanding by reformulation.** After a block, the student explains it in their own words, "as to a classmate". Only then a short application question.
3. **Error: hint, second try, then the answer.** Name what is right, give one targeted hint, let the student try again. The answer is given only after two failed attempts, with its explanation, followed by a twin question (same idea, other example) to check.
4. **Unclear answer: ask once, then judge.** An answer that cannot be judged (ambiguous, cut off, badly transcribed) is neither right nor wrong: the tutor asks the student to clarify, with no hint and no attempt counted. Only once; on the next turn it must judge.
5. **Re-read what must be learnt word for word.** At the end of an explanation block, the tutor re-reads the one key sentence (definition, or formula with the meaning of each term), announced by "Je relis :", exactly as on the board. When the student asks to hear it again ("répète", "عاود", "ما سمعتش"), the tutor re-reads it identically and adds nothing; this is not an attempt. "Je ne comprends pas" is different: change the angle. "se répète" is a physics answer, not a request.
6. **Read a problem statement twice before any calculation.** First reading: "what is this exercise about?" in one sentence. Second, active reading: the data with their units, what is asked, and the verb of the instruction (calculer, justifier, montrer que, déduire). Neither reading is graded (`<eval>aucune</eval>`); solving starts after.
7. **Prerequisite gap: open a parenthesis, then come back.** When the error comes from a competency of earlier years (TC, 1re bac, collège — e.g. derivatives, projecting a vector, cell structure), the tutor says "On ouvre une parenthèse : [titre]", re-explains it in 3 to 5 sentences with an example from the original level, re-reads the key sentence, asks one verification question, then says "On ferme la parenthèse" and re-asks the course question unchanged, with fresh attempts. Never nested, at most 3 per session. The candidate prerequisites per chapter live in `backend/data/curriculum/prerequis.json` (each with its official source) and are offered to the model only after a first failed attempt; the model declares one with `<prerequis>id</prerequis>`, ids outside the chapter's list are ignored. Opening/closing is sent to the screen (`parenthese` event) and the closing is recorded in `student_answer_history` with `source = "parenthese"`.
8. **Stay inside the programme's perimeter.** For chapters that have a sheet in `backend/data/curriculum/perimetre.json` (first one: dipôle RC), the prompt receives the chapter's notions, the methods expected at the bac, what is admitted without proof, and the terms not to use with what to say instead (no Laplace, no complex impedance, no Bode for the RC…). After each reply, `perimetre.detecter` re-reads the displayed text; in this first phase it only logs to `data/perimetre_journal.jsonl` (count with `scripts/decompte_perimetre.py`), it never corrects. Enforcement comes only once the lists have been checked against real replies. For the COURSES, the same perimeter is enforced before publication by `controler_cadre.py` against `backend/data/curriculum/cadre_reference_2bac_pc.json` (capacities of the official cadre de référence, formulas as written and said, out-of-scope words) — skill `cours-concentration`, step 8.
9. **Warm and demanding.** Praise precisely what is earned and why; name what is missing without detours; never move on while the student is approximate. "Almost" only when it is true.
10. **Write as the marking scheme reads** (learnt from real Moroccan teachers, November 2026, like rules 11–15). Every calculation: the relation with the statement's own symbols, the numerical substitution, the result, the unit — each one earns marks; a single numerical substitution at the end; answer the question asked and nothing more. A calculated answer missing the relation, the substitution or the unit is at best `partiel`.
11. **Check before validating.** Compare a numerical result with the chapter's reference order of magnitude, and redo it by a second route when one exists. An absurd result is pointed out, never written down.
12. **Read the axis before the graph.** In front of a curve, an oscillogram or a photo: what is on the horizontal axis, in which unit, what one division is worth — before reading any value.
13. **Recognise the standard case.** Spot the statement's code word ("réfléchi", "deux milieux", "t₁ inconnu"…), name the case, draw the situation, apply the case's method, then vary one parameter ("and if the receiver moves away?").
14. **A definition is built by three questions** — what? where? which characteristics? — then re-read word for word (rule 5).
15. **A multiple-choice item is read to its last word** and justified by the word that decides it.
16. **Chapter experience, served when it helps.** `backend/data/curriculum/experience_profs.json` holds, per chapter (keys of `perimetre.json`), what teachers repeat, the frequent mistakes with the correcting move, the standard cases with their method, reference values, approximations teachers say that must NOT be repeated, and what was excluded as out of programme. `experience_profs.py` serves it in pieces: the insistences and "ne dis pas" when explaining, the standard cases and reference values when solving starts, the mistakes only after a first failed attempt. Standard cases are all named, but only those whose clues appear in the student's text get their full method (keeps the block short). `questions_eleves` holds questions real students ask under the teachers' videos, with the correct answer; `bloc_questions` serves the two closest to the student's message, so a free question is answered the way it should be. After each reply `experience_profs.detecter` logs repeated approximations to `data/experience_journal.jsonl` (observation only). Every entry must be checked against `perimetre.json`; `test_experience_profs.py` fails if an out-of-programme term is taught. First chapter: `ondes_mecaniques`.

17. **The spoken darija script is simple and uses only what the voice was trained on** (user rule, 7 November 2026). The full procedure — French for equations, operations, numbers, units, « en fonction de » and exam words; checking, generating and verifying the voice — is the `voix-du-cours` skill. Applies to every `speech_text.mixed` of a course and to anything the voice reads aloud. That includes cutting every all-French sentence longer than ten words with commas: in one long piece the voice loses its way and mumbles. This holds in the course and in the live replies to free questions. The voice model (v4, `ferdaous04/academy-v4-entrainement`) learnt from 38 852 sentences of Moroccan teachers (`zouhairessaihi/academy-v4-selection`, `selection_train.jsonl`, 28 % of the words French): a word it never heard is mispronounced, and a word the students do not know is not understood either.
    - **French only for three things:** the programme's technical terms (l'onde, la perturbation, la célérité, le retard, le milieu, le front, l'élongation, l'aspect, la source, la propagation, transversale, longitudinale, la sensibilité, division, l'oscilloscope, le récepteur…), the **numbers** said the way BIOF teachers say them in class (deux, zéro virgule deux, trois cent quarante, mille cinq cents — the corpus has deux 4 675 times, zéro 3 549, virgule 418), and the simple French words every student already uses (donc, alors, voilà, d'accord, c'est, bien, déjà, normalement).
    - **Any other French word goes into simple darija.** Not in the corpus and not known by most students, so neither the voice nor the student gets it: « la bouée » (0 times; heard back as « la boue »), « la queue » (0), « la manip » (0), « clap » (0), « impulsion » (0), « le barème » (0), « l'énoncé » (0), « trajet » (0), « divisée » (0), « oscillogramme » (0), « l'échographie » (0). Say instead, with words the corpus does contain: « واحد الكورة صغيرة فوق الما » (bouée), « اللخر ديال la perturbation » (queue), « فالتجربة » (manip), « كنضرب بيدي » / « le signal » (clap, impulsion), « النقط » (barème), « السؤال » / « التمرين » (énoncé), « المسافة » (trajet), « مقسومة على » (divisée par).
    - **Symbols as the corpus writes them:** « teta » (335 times; « thêta » 0), « tau » (426), « y grec ديال M », « delta t », « lambda ».
    - **Darija from the corpus, short sentences.** Prefer the words teachers use most: يعني، دابا، إذن، هادي/هذا، شنو، كيفاش، علاش، واش، باش، شحال، مثلا، مزيان، بحال، ملي، **يلا** (3 505 times, rather than « إلا », 492), كيساوي/كتساوي. Avoid rare, literary or long glued forms (« فالمراية », « مزحزحين », « كتحسم », « وكيبعدو » never appear): one idea per sentence, the verb near the start, no stacked « و…ف… » prefixes.
    - **Never add tashkil to fix a word.** The corpus has none; harakat push the voice off its training and make it worse. A darija word that seems to need vowels is replaced by a simpler, more common one (or said in French if it is a programme term). The live tutor follows the same rule (`llm_service.py`, [RÈGLE DE SÉCURITÉ — PRONONCIATION ET FRANÇAIS]).
    - **Check before generating.** Every French word of the script and every rare darija word is looked up in `selection_train.jsonl`. A French word seen fewer than 20 times that is not a programme term is replaced. A programme term rarely seen (« l'élongation » 62, « réfléchie » 1, « l'écho » 0) stays, because the student must hear it for the bac, but it is said with its article and once explained in darija (« réfléchie، يعني كترجع »). The full count for the waves example is in the shared notes (`voix-dataset-v4.md`).

18. **Whatever the tutor says about a simulation, the simulation shows it at that moment** (user rule, 8 November 2026). A phase that is described (« M monte, descend, revient à sa place », « voilà le retard τ », « toute la perturbation est sur la corde ») is not described over a scene that already finished: the tutor replays the simulation and freezes it on that very instant (`play_until {moment}`), then points at the landmark (`highlight`). Several phases said in a row are shown one after the other (monte → freeze, descend → freeze, revient → freeze); a quantity being defined is shown frozen where it is largest or readable (l'élongation: M at its top, with the y arrow). So the key instants are **extracted from the simulation first**: each simulation exposes named `moments` computed from its physics (`simulations-au-tableau` skill), and the course script (`concentration.pointage` of a slide, `{mot, commande: "play_until", parametres: {moment}}`) or the live tutor targets them by name, never by guessed seconds. In the concentration player (`CourseFocusStage.tsx`) a command cue leaves 0,9 s before its word, the time for the professor's hand to reach the button; measured on the rope slide, each freeze lands within half a second of its word.

19. **A variant is shown by the tutor, and the student's own result is interpreted on the spot** (user rule, 8 November 2026). If a simulation can be varied, the tutor varies it himself after explaining (M₂ twice as far, the bottom lower) to give the comparison. When the student varies it himself, he gets an answer about HIS result: what changed, with the values, and why (the law of the course); then one question on that result: vrai/faux if he just made a mistake, QCM by default, a direct question once he answers right. It is generated at that moment (DeepSeek, `app/services/simulation_reaction.py`, endpoint `/course-player/sim-reaction`), said at once by the live voice under rule 17, and the student stays on the same slide. Test: `app/tests/test_simulation_reaction.py`.

20. **Questions are shown at once and every answer gets the tutor's voice** (user rule, 8 November 2026). In the concentration player the slide's question and exercises open as soon as the voice ends (« ✏️ À toi », no hunting for a button). Each answer — a QCM choice, each vrai/faux statement, a written answer — gets a reaction generated at that moment (DeepSeek, `app/services/reponse_eleve.py`, endpoint `/course-player/answer-reaction`, about 2 s) and said by the live voice: right → what is right and why; wrong at the first try → what is right in it, one targeted hint, no answer, the wrong choice is struck out and he tries again (rule 3); wrong again → the answer and its justification. When the exercise carries its key, the verdict is imposed on the model, which only explains it. Test: `app/tests/test_reponse_eleve.py`. **Every possible answer is prepared in advance** (user rule, 8 November 2026): each question of a course carries its key (`question.correct`, options shuffled so the right one is not always first), and for EVERY option the tutor's reaction is written beforehand with the same prompt and stored in the slide — `concentration.reponses[cle][option]`: `juste` for the right option, `1` (hint) and `2` (answer) for each wrong one — with its voice track generated by the course voice (`/media/audio/courses/<deck>/reponses/`). The player plays the prepared reaction at once, with no server; only a free written answer still goes to DeepSeek and the live voice. A new course is not finished until all its questions have their prepared reactions and tracks. **Then the course moves on by itself** (user rule, 12 November 2026): once the student has answered (or skipped) and the reaction — and the demonstration, its board closed — is said, ~1.5 s later the player goes to the next question, or to the next slide, unless the student touched the screen meanwhile; a reprise cycle and the end of a block stay on « Suite → ». The tutor also reads each statement aloud when it opens (`enonce_audio`, else `enonce_dit`, else the statement normalised), and a discreet « Passer » records `passee: true` (neither right nor an error). **The body answers too** (user rule, 8 November 2026): with the voice, the 3D tutor expresses the reaction — right at the first try → `celebrate` (arms up, sparks), right after the hint → `encourage`, wrong at the first try → `wrong` (shakes his head, 💡), wrong again → `encourage`, an answer he cannot judge → `think`, a result the student got by himself in the simulation → `surprise` (`AIAvatar emotion`, `CourseFocusStage.ressentir`).

21. **Name the view before using it** (user rule, 8 November 2026). Students do not know what a « vue de dessus », a « vue de côté », a « profil », a « coupe » or a graph « y en fonction de x » shows, nor what « transversale / longitudinale » means in the picture. Before any figure or simulation with several views, the tutor says, in one sentence each, what each view is and where the eye is (« la vue de dessus : كنشوفو الما من الفوق » ; « la vue de côté : من الجنب، باش نشوفو الكورة كتطلع وكتهبط »), pointing at it; before « transversale » or « longitudinale » he shows WHERE the point moves and WHERE the wave goes, then names it. This holds in the course scripts (the slide's spoken text starts with the views, with a `pointage` on each) and in the live replies (the simulation's `ce_qui_se_voit` names its views; the tutor repeats them when he points there). Example: `wx_o1_bouee`.

22. **The reprise cycle: nobody moves on before it is understood, and nothing is explained twice the same way** (user rule, 8 November 2026). In the concentration player, when the slide's questions are all right, after the bravo « Suite → » turns green and the student goes on when he wants (user rule, 11 November 2026: nothing moves by itself). When one is wrong (after the hint and the second try), the tutor stays on the SAME slide and re-explains ANOTHER way — with the full context: what he already said, the question, the wrong answers, the methods already tried, the methods that work for this student (skill `suivi-des-acquis`), the simulation's instants and landmarks — while piloting the figure (`play_until`, `highlight`); then he asks a TWIN question (same competence, other form or values, one try). Right → next slide. Wrong → next cycle with another entry: 1 = the simulation frozen step by step, 2 = a worked example with other numbers, 3 = the answer itself, justified, written in the notebook. **Three cycles at most**; after the third the competence is marked NOT ACQUIRED with the methods tried, and the student moves on. The student may leave the slide by hand at any cycle: the competence is then marked NOT ACQUIRED too (`cycle<n>_abandon`), so the free mode and the next course take it back. Code: `app/services/reexplication.py`, endpoint `/course-player/reexplication`, `CourseFocusStage.lancerCycle`; test `app/tests/test_reexplication.py`.

23. **The student can raise a hand at any moment — in writing, by voice or with an image** (user rule, 8 November 2026). The « 🙋 Question » button of the concentration player pauses the course and the simulation, and takes a written question, a microphone recording (transcribed in darija by the STT, French technical terms kept) or an image (a photo of an exercise, a diagram, a notebook page: text read by the OCR). The tutor answers WITHOUT leaving the slide, with the whole context — what he just said, what the simulation shows (`ce_qui_se_voit`), its instants and landmarks (he points while answering), and the plan of the course (slides before and after). **The answer is never a text bubble: he explains it at the board, by voice; the text is shown only on demand** (« 📄 Voir le texte ») (user rule, 9 November 2026). He first classes the question against the COURSE (`categorie`):
   - **`diapo`** — the notion of this slide, or one already seen earlier in this course → a board opens under the figure (which stays visible for his gestures) and he writes 2–6 short French lines (`tableau`: keywords, definition, relations) at the pace of his voice, pointing at each line; 3 to 6 spoken sentences, then back to the course.
   - **`suivante`** — a notion of this course taught in a slide still to come → he does not explain it: a pre-recorded sentence (« هاد la notion غادي نشوفوها قريب… دابا نركزو على هاد الجزء ») while the board shows the notion and the title of that slide, then the course resumes on the current slide.
   - **`prerequis`** — a notion from previous years or a basic one needed here (vitesse moyenne, période, conversion…) → a brush erases the board in three passes during a pre-recorded sentence (« واخا، هادي حاجة من الأساس، نبداو بيها اللول. خليني نمسح السبورة. »): that time is used to write the explanation (`/question-eleve/explication`) and compute its voice; then he explains it from scratch on the clean board, with an everyday example, and links it to the current slide.
   - **`hors_cours`** — another chapter, another course or another subject → he does not answer; a pre-recorded sentence says so simply and the course resumes.
   **After every explanation (`diapo` and `prerequis`) he asks whether it is understood and checks it with a small exercise** (user rule, 9 November 2026): when the voice of the explanation ends, the pre-recorded « دابا، واش فهمتي مزيان؟ يلا نتأكدو بواحد التمرين صغير. » plays and the exercise is written on the board, under the lines of the explanation. The LLM writes it with the explanation (`exercice`) and picks the type by the case: **association** (notions ↔ definitions, quantities ↔ units or relations, 3–4 pairs: touch left, then right), **vrai_faux** (properties, misconceptions, 3 statements), **qcm** (a relation, a result, an interpretation). One attempt; the correction is written, the tutor reacts by voice (`parole_juste` / `parole_faux`), and the answer is recorded in the acquis (`cle: question_libre_exercice`, judged). An unusable exercise is dropped (`valider_exercice`), the explanation stays.
   An exercise on an image → the case, the relation and the first step, never the final answer. Pre-recorded sentences: `frontend/public/media/audio/reactions/{tableau-effacer,notion-plus-tard,hors-cours,verifier-compris}.mp3` (course voice, recorded on the trial kernel); they work for every course, nothing to redo per course. The question is recorded in the acquis as `question_libre` (not judged), so the free mode knows what the student asked. Code: `app/services/question_eleve.py`, endpoints `/course-player/question-eleve` and `/course-player/question-eleve/explication` (public page: same under `/public/cours-ondes/`), `CourseFocusStage.leverLaMain` / `envoyerQuestion` / `TableauDuProf`; tests `app/tests/test_question_eleve.py`, `app/tests/test_question_au_tableau.py`.
24. **A normal conversation is not a request for an explanation** (user rule, 9 November 2026). The tutor explains only when the student asks for it — never after each message. « merci », « شكرا », « مزيان », « واخا », « d'accord », « j'ai compris », « c'est bon », « bonjour », « salam » get what a person would answer: one short warm sentence, then the course goes on (it resumes if it was paused; if the slide's voice was finished, its questions open, or the next slide comes). No board, no re-explanation, no exercise.
   - **Instant, without the server:** the player recognises these words itself (`motSocial` in `CourseFocusStage.tsx`: six words at most, no question mark, no question word, no negation such as « pas compris » / « ما فهمتش ») and plays a pre-recorded sentence of the course voice: `social-merci.mp3` (« العفو! يلا نكملو الدرس. »), `social-ok.mp3` (« مزيان بزاف! يلا نكملو. »), `social-salut.mp3` (« أهلا، مرحبا بيك! يلا نكملو الدرس ديالنا. »).
   - **Anything else that is small talk** (« كيف داير أستاذ », a remark, a kind word) is classed `conversation` by the LLM: one or two spoken sentences, nothing written, then the course goes on.
   - A message that says the student did NOT understand, or that asks (« عاود », « explique », « علاش »), is a request: rule 23 applies.
25. **The student never waits in silence** (user rule, 9 November 2026 — measured: the answer's voice took 14 to 18 s because the whole audio was computed before the first sound). When a real question is sent, a pre-recorded sentence starts at once (« مزيان، سؤال زوين. » / « إيه، هادي نقطة مهمة. ») while the LLM answers (2–5 s). The spoken answer is then cut into pieces (`decouperParole`: a first piece of 25–70 characters, then up to 120, then up to 170) synthesised in parallel on the two GPU cards: the first piece is heard after about 3 s, each next piece is ready before the previous one ends, and the board lines follow the progress over all the pieces. Any new spoken reply longer than two sentences must use `preparerMorceaux`, never one single `/tts/speak` call.

26. **Respect the limit of what the student can take in, then fix it in long-term memory** (user rule, 11 November 2026 — « on veut des résultats, pas seulement une belle plateforme »). About four new elements at a time: one new idea per slide, blocks of 3–4 slides, then the notebook (written, confirmed), then the student retrieves it from memory with the notebook closed, then one exercise. On signs of saturation (two wrong in a row, too-fast answers, long inactivity) the tutor stops explaining and moves to the notebook, the exercise or a break. Each session opens with a short spaced recall of the same subject only. The pace is the student's: nothing moves on by itself — except, right after an answered question, the move to the next question or slide once the reaction (and demonstration) is over (user rule, 12 November 2026, rule 20). Full procedure and data: skill `cours-concentration` (« Memory and rhythm ») and `suivi-des-acquis` (sections 6–8).

27. **Never ask about what has not been taught yet, and do not question every slide** (user rule, 12 November 2026 — « ici on pose des questions d'ATP alors que l'élève ne sait pas encore ce que veut dire ATP »). The order of the course is the order of what may be asked: at any moment the tutor may only use the notions already explained **in this course up to this slide** and in the courses already done. A word that has not been introduced (ATP, mitochondrie, impédance…) never appears — not in a statement, not in an option, not even in a wrong option — before the slide that teaches it. A notion of a later slide is announced, not asked (rule 23, `suivante`).
    - **Earlier years are allowed, but not as a test.** A competence of collège, TC or 1re bac may be used, because it is supposed to be known; it is never what the question grades, and **many students did not finish the earlier programme**. When a question depends on such a notion, the tutor first says it in one sentence (« rappel : … »), and a mistake on it opens a parenthesis (rule 7) instead of counting as a failed attempt.
    - **Not one question per slide.** The student is accompanied, not examined: a question exists only where something just explained is worth checking, and it stays a general question on the idea (what it is, what changes, what one sees), never a detail or a term learnt elsewhere. A slide that only sets the scene, names the views (rule 21) or continues a reasoning carries **no question**; prefer one real question per block of 3–4 slides, asked after the explanation — no prediction step any more (user rule, 13 November 2026). Exercises count too: **one « À toi » board per block** (the block's question + at most one exercise), never a board on every slide — the other exercises wait in `exercices_fin_de_cours` for the end-of-course step (user rule, 13 November 2026, « veuillez les espacer »). **Where it goes:** the block's single question is the **last** one — after the explanation, just before the block's exercise — And the voice says it once: a recorded slide track that ends on the question is the question, so a slide without a written question must not end on one (applied to the 19 courses on 13 November 2026: 478 questions removed, 465 tracks redone — `docs/audit-questions-progression.md`).
    - **Checked when the course is built:** every statement, option and recall question is re-read against the slides that come BEFORE it (and the courses already done); anything that needs a later notion is rewritten or moved after that slide. Same rule for the live tutor, the reprise cycle's twin questions (rule 22) and the small exercise after a free question (rule 23).

28. **Show it moving, let the student play with it — not a table, not a list** (user rule, 14 November 2026 — « il fallait penser à les créer avec des petites animations simulations pour ancrer la notion chez l'élève au lieu de le faire sous forme écrit ou tableau » ; « faire des manips dans l'air, montrer la célérité et les différents éléments, changer la température et voir les changements »). When a slide compares values (célérités of the media, half-lives, time constants, pH of solutions…), shows how a quantity depends on a parameter (température, tension, R, C, concentration…), or describes a process in steps, the board shows a **small chalk animation** instead of a table or a list of bullets: the values appear as the result of what is seen (the sound reaches the end of the steel first, then the water, then the air), and **one or two sliders let the student change the parameter and see the effect at once** (air temperature → the sound arrives earlier or later, its v written beside). Simple, useful, pleasant: one idea, one button (start / pause), at most two sliders, the numbers written at the place where they happen, no menu; the voice drives it (`play_until`, `set_parameters`, `highlight`) on its words. A table stays only for the bilan sheet and the end-of-chapter map, or for data the exam gives as a table. Reference: `ondes-tableau/milieux.html` (course of the sound in steel, water, air; air temperature slider; « air frappé plus fort » arrives with the air: the amplitude does not change v), on slide `wx_c2_facteurs`. Build rules: skill `cours-concentration` step 3.

29. **One voice at a time: the voice heard is always the one of the screen in front of the student** (user rule, 14 November 2026 — « j'ai cliqué sur la question, l'audio de l'explication reste et s'ajoute à l'audio de la question ; normalement il doit arrêter l'explication et laisser l'audio de la question ; si l'écran est fermé, l'audio doit aussi stopper »). Every sound of the course goes through the single-voice registry `frontend/src/components/course/voixUnique.ts` (`inscrireVoix(el, coupe)`): the slide track, the tutor voice (statements, reactions, demonstrations, social clips) and the memory-cycle voice (`memoire/voix.ts`). When one starts, all the others stop at once; the slide track that was cut is marked paused and **resumes where it stopped when the screen that cut it is closed**. Closing a screen (× of the « À toi » board, of a bubble, switching bubble, « Revenir à la scène ») silences its voice at once (`fermerPanneau` / `basculerPanneau`). The cases: question opened during the explanation; bubble (cahier, explication) opened or closed; student question; result / reaction; demonstration; observation question; bloc end (cahier, rappel). Any new player (a new `new Audio`, a new `<audio>`) MUST register with `inscrireVoix`, never play on its own.

30. **Let the student notice it himself: the observation question** (user rule, 14 November 2026 — on `ms_t3_sans_dioxygene`: « quand le tuteur passe en absent, il enchaîne tout de suite : l'élève n'a pas le temps d'observer ni de remarquer lui-même que la chaleur retardée a disparu »). Where the student must SEE something in a simulation by himself — a disappearance (no delayed heat without O₂), what changes when a slider or a setting moves, a comparison between two visible cases — the tutor stops his voice just before he says it, asks one short question (« Qu'est-ce qui a disparu ? »), and **goes silent**: the student looks. After each silence (8 s by default) one progressive hint comes (one or two, from vague to precise, the tutor may point at a landmark), then three answer cards (or Vrai / Faux) in the same band, under the figure, never over it. The student answers with the **answer micro** — a separate button, shown only during an observation question, held to speak (push-to-talk) while the tutor holds his hand to his ear; what he says is transcribed and judged as the ANSWER to this question (`/course-player/answer-reaction`), never as a free question (rule 23 is untouched) — or he clicks a card. Every answer gets its feedback, said and written: right → what had to be seen; wrong at the first try → what is wrong and where to look (the card is struck out), he tries again; wrong again → the answer; not understood → « redis-le ». It is recorded in the acquis (`cle: 'obs'`, `type: 'observation'`). Then the voice of the course resumes where it stopped, and its next sentence confirms the answer. **Sparingly**: at most one per slide, zero to two per course, only on a slide where the simulation itself shows the thing to notice and the voice says it right after the stop — never on a definition, a calculation, a formula, a bilan, never on what has not been taught yet (rule 27), and never instead of the block's « ✏️ À toi » question (rule 20). If the simulation writes the conclusion on screen, it must be able to keep quiet until the answer (`set_parameters {conclusions: false}`, then `true`), otherwise the answer is in front of the student; the slide subtitle is hidden for the same reason while the question is open. Data and building: skill `cours-concentration`, step 5 (`concentration.observation`). Code: `CourseFocusStage.tsx` (`ouvrirObservation`, `BandeObservation`), the ear gesture `AIAvatar mainOreille` → `Professor3D holdEar`. Reference: `svt_ch2_role_muscle_strie_squelettique` / `ms_t3_sans_dioxygene` (`chaleur.html` accepts `conclusions`).

31. **The notebook draws what can be drawn** (user rule, 14 November 2026 — « dans À écrire dans le cahier il n'y a que du texte, aucune courbe, aucun graphe, aucun croquis ; ajoutez quelques courbes, schémas, croquis avec des légendes, seulement les éléments essentiels »). What the student copies is the essential text **plus, when the notion can be drawn, a curve, a diagram or a sketch with captions** — the curve u_C(t) with τ and 0,63·E, the rope with λ, the pH axis with pKa, the decay N(t) with t½, the variation table, the sarcomere at rest and contracted. A teacher's board: few strokes, axes named with their units, the course's values, one caption saying what to see; not a drawing on every slide (about one per block, none for a definition, a map or a bilan), only what has already been taught (rule 27). Data `cahier_figures` on the slide (`concentration`) and on the block, rendered in chalk (`FigureCahier.tsx`); build rules: skill `cours-concentration` step 5, format `docs/audit-cahier/FORMAT.md`.

32. **Realistic drawings, in colour: a living thing or a real object is never a poor chalk sketch** (user rule, 14 November 2026 — « au lieu de mettre un croquis de mauvaise qualité, veuillez mettre un dessin avec des couleurs pour le muscle ; toujours chercher le réalisme dans les croquis »). In an animation, a muscle, a bone, a cell, a mitochondria, an organ, a beaker, a hammer, an ear, a spring, a circuit component… is PAINTED on the canvas before the chalk (gradients, shadow, texture: fibres of a muscle, ivory of a bone, pearly tendon, glass of a beaker with its liquid), with true shapes and proportions, the way `svt/role_muscle_strie_squelettique-tableau/chaleur.html` (`peindreMuscle`, `tendon`) and `consommation.html` paint the muscle. The chalk keeps what a teacher writes: labels, arrows, axes, curves, formulas, values. Two lines for a muscle, a stick for a bone, a rectangle for a beaker are refused. Keep it simple (few details, the essential ones), but recognisable at first sight; a drawing for the notebook (rule 31) follows the same rule.

33. **One thing at a time, at the student's pace: the principle first, then each step slowly, then let him find the result** (user rule, 14 November 2026, on Griffith's experiment — « il donne tout d'une manière rapide, l'élève ne peut pas le suivre ; à chaque expérience il doit donner le temps nécessaire pour voir la manip et la comprendre, et ensuite les résultats ; plusieurs infos successives risquent de ne pas être suivies »). A slide never shows several experiments, results or ideas in one go. For an experiment or a set of experiments: (1) explain the PRINCIPLE and the set-up first (what is injected / measured / changed, and why), alone on its slide or freeze; (2) a short check question on the principle (rule 27: only what was just taught); (3) each experiment (case, trial) gets its own moment: the animation plays it, freezes, the voice says what is seen and the result of THAT one only, and leaves a pause to look (`play_until` per experiment, a silence of a few seconds after each result); (4) the decisive experiment — the one whose result carries the conclusion (Griffith's 4th mouse) — is NOT given: the tutor plays the manip, stops before the result, and asks an observation question (rule 30: free answer by micro, then three cards or vrai/faux after a delay, hints) so the student finds it himself; only then the result and the conclusion are said. (5) after the last experiment, and only then, a **recap screen shows all the experiments together** with their results and the conclusion (user rule, 14 November 2026 — « séparer ces expériences, chaque expérience apparaît avec ses résultats, bien la comprendre ; après la dernière expérience, on les montre toutes dans un seul écran »): the side-by-side comparison comes once each case is understood, never as the first view (model: Griffith, `ig_t1_griffith` → `ig_t1b…e` → `ig_t1f_bilan`). In the animation, the results and conclusions of later steps are hidden until their moment (`conclusions: false` during the question). Same for a demonstration, a reasoning in several steps, a table of cases: one line at a time, each said and seen before the next.

34. **Start from zero: the student knows nothing, prerequisites are taught, not assumed, and each notion is explained twice in two different ways** (user rule, 14 November 2026 — « considérer que l'élève ne connaît rien, on commence dès zéro ; chaque notion doit être expliquée et réexpliquée d'une autre manière pour être ancrée ; le cours a commencé par la mitose et les chromosomes alors que la plupart des élèves ne connaissent pas encore ces notions malgré qu'ils les aient étudiées »). A notion of an earlier year (mitose, chromosome, ADN, vecteur, dérivée, mole, quantité de matière, tension…) used by the course is never just named as known: the course opens with short recall slides that TEACH it from zero (what it is, a picture or animation, one example), then uses it. Every new notion is said a first way (definition + animation), then a second, different way (an everyday image, an analogy, a counter-example or the same idea seen from another view) before moving on. When rebuilding a course, check each slide: which words would a student who forgot everything not understand? Each of them is taught before, on its own slide. New or rewritten scripts are written in the course's staging copy `backend/data/courses/en_attente/<deck>.json` (not served) with `audio` removed on every slide whose `speech_text.mixed` changed, until the Bodi voice is recorded; then the staging copy replaces the live course.

35. **Tabulated data: the animation first, then the table** (user rule, 14 November 2026, on Chargaff's table — « au lieu du tableau, une animation qui montre les bases azotées dans un style réaliste et en couleur, qui compte les bases de chaque espèce et fait voir que A ≈ T et G ≈ C ; ensuite seulement l'animation se transforme en tableau »). A table of data the student reads — measures, counts, results of an experiment (Chargaff's percentages, cells counted over time, pH measured, nuclei left day by day, values read on an instrument) — is never shown first as a table. The board first PRODUCES the values in a realistic, coloured animation (rule 32): the experiment or the counting is seen (the bases of each species painted and sorted into jars, the cells dividing in their dish, the pH-meter reading each beaker), the numbers appear where they are produced (counters that climb, the display of the instrument), and the comparison that carries the notion is SEEN before it is computed (jars A and T filled to the same level). Only then does the animation turn into the table: the scene fades, each number flies into its cell, the computed columns (A/T, C/G…) and the conclusion are written last. One species / one trial slowly with its comparison, the others faster, then the table. Moments on the voice (`play_until`): one per step (the first count, each comparison, the other cases, the table, the conclusion). The tables that stay tables: sign, variation, position and avancement tables (reasoning tools), unit and method tables, the bilan sheet and the chapter map, and data an exam statement gives as a table. Reference: view `chargaff` of `svt/notion_information_genetique-tableau/adn.html` on slide `ig_s2_chargaff`. Build rules: skill `cours-concentration` step 3.

36. **Everything written on screen is easy to read** (user rule, 16 November 2026). On a slide, a simulation, a drawing or the notebook: dark bold text on a light object, white or a bright chalk colour on the dark board, never pale grey for what the lesson is about, text centred in its band and inside its shape, nothing overlapping, nothing spilling out of its container. Details for simulations: `simulations-au-tableau`, rules 9 and 10.

37. **A demonstration goes to the end, every step written — never jump to the answer** (user rule, 16 November 2026 — « dans les démonstrations, il le détaille jusqu'au bout, ne pas passer les étapes et donner directement la solution »). Applies to every demonstration: the board after a question or an exercise (`demonstration.lignes`), a proof on a slide, a worked example, a cahier proof, a block `exercice`, a reaction that corrects (`ecrit` / `parole`).
    - **Start from what is given and name the tool**: the data of the statement, then the property, formula or theorem used, said with its conditions (« f est continue et strictement croissante sur [a ; b], donc… », « u > 0 sur I, donc ln u est dérivable »).
    - **One step per line, nothing done in the head**: each transformation, substitution, simplification, sign change, factorisation or intermediate value has its own line (« 6/(−3) = −2 » is written, not jumped over); each line says WHAT is done and, when it is not obvious, WHY (« car… », « d'après… »).
    - **Go to the end**: the last line answers exactly the question asked (the value with its unit in physics-chemistry, the set of solutions, the conclusion sentence « donc f est croissante sur I », the geometric interpretation when it is asked), as the marking scheme reads it (rule 10). A demonstration that stops at « … = −2 » when the question was « résoudre » ends with « S = ]−2 ; +∞[ ».
    - **Too long for one board** (about 8 lines): split it into two boards or two demonstrations (first the method, then the calculation) — never shorten it by skipping steps.
    - **The voice says every line** (`parole`, voice rules of rule 17; every `mot` an exact extract of it); a new or longer demonstration loses its `audio` until the voice is recorded.
    - **Shown one line at a time** (rule 33): each line appears on its word in the voice, never the whole proof at once; on a slide, a proof in several steps uses one `concentration.centre` entry (or one `pointage`) per line, not a block pasted at once.
    - **Physics-chemistry** (user rule, 17 November 2026 — teachers' courses compared with ours, « prendre en considération les skills de démonstration au fond »): a calculation is written as the marking scheme reads it (rule 10): the literal relation with the symbols of the statement, then the substitution with the units converted on their own line, then the result with its unit and the right number of significant figures. A relation is never posted without the line it comes from: « τ = SM/v car v = d/Δt avec d = SM et Δt = t_M − t_S », « λ = v·T car pendant T l'onde parcourt λ », « n = λ₀/λ car n = c/v et v/c = λ/λ₀ ». A differential equation is established to the end: the law used (loi d'additivité des tensions, convention récepteur with the arrows drawn, u_R = R·i, u_C = q/C, i = dq/dt, u_L = L·di/dt + r·i; for decay « noyaux désintégrés pendant dt = N(t) − N(t+dt) = −dN »), each substitution on its line, the equation in its canonical form with the constant named (τ = RC, τ = L/(R+r), 1/λ). A solution is checked to the end: it is substituted in the equation (derivative written, the terms that cancel shown), then the initial condition gives the constant (N(0) = N₀·e⁰ = N₀, u_C(0) = 0), then the constant is identified. A special value is computed, not stated: N(τ) = N₀·e^(−λ·1/λ) = N₀·e⁻¹ ≈ 0,37·N₀ ; N(n·t½) = N₀·e^(−n·ln 2) = N₀/2ⁿ ; u_C(τ) = E(1 − e⁻¹) ≈ 0,63·E ; the tangent at the origin is written as an equation before reading where it cuts the asymptote.

38. **Write the condition and the reason, as the bac requires** (user rule, 16 November 2026 — teachers' courses compared with ours: the maths equivalent of the physical state in a chemical equation). Every written conclusion carries its justification (« f est dérivable sur ℝ car c'est un polynôme », « f est continue sur I car elle est dérivable sur I »); every formula of a summary, a fiche, a cahier or a bilan keeps its domain and its conditions (intervalle « sur I », u > 0, u ≠ 0, z ≠ 0, x ∈ D_f, « + C »/« + k » for primitives, « dans un repère orthonormé » for the symmetry about y = x); the notations are those the student writes at the bac (vector arrow over AB, the bar of the conjugate, « x → a » / « x → a⁺ » under lim, « n → +∞ », ∀x ∈ I, arg z ≡ θ [2π], f′_d(a)), and the standard interpretation sentences are written, not only said (« la droite d'équation x = a est une asymptote verticale à (C_f) », « (C_f) coupe l'axe des abscisses en un point unique d'abscisse α »). In physics-chemistry the same rule reads: every law is written with its conditions of validity and its units (« θ = λ/a, θ en rad, valable pour θ petit », « λ = v·T dans un milieu homogène », the physical states (aq), (l), (s), (g) in an equation), every convention is drawn before it is used (convention récepteur, direction of i, arrow of each voltage, the sign of the charge on each plate), and the standard bac sentences are written (« le milieu est dispersif car la célérité dépend de la fréquence », « le régime permanent est atteint au bout de 5τ »). Audits: `docs/audit-prof/maths.md`, `docs/audit-prof/maths-autres-profs.md`, `docs/audit-prof/physique.md`.

39. **Ce que dit le professeur, la simulation le montre en même temps — and the pointer lands on the object he names** (user rule, 17 November 2026, on a capture of the slide « Le dioxygène, dernier du relais »: « le professeur parle mais la simulation ne reflète pas ce qu'il dit — quand il parle de l'oxydation de NADH,H⁺, on ne voit pas NADH,H⁺ céder ses électrons à T1 ; la baguette se pose parfois sur un autre objet que celui qu'il nomme »). Rule 18 made precise, for every slide and every live explanation:
   - **Every process the tutor names has its own animated moment, and the command leaves with the sentence that names it.** « NADH,H⁺ cède ses électrons à T1 » = a moment where NADH,H⁺ arrives at T1 and its two electrons leave it (`taxi_decharge`); « T1 → T2 → T3 → T4 » = one moment per hop (`e_t2`, `e_t3`, `electrons_passent`); « ½ O₂ + 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻ → H₂O » = the dioxygen arriving at T4, receiving, water forming; pumping, return through the sphère pédonculée, ADP + Pi → ATP — each its own `play_until`, sent at the word that says it (`concentration.pointage`), long enough to be seen while the sentence lasts (`duree_s` when the phrase is short). What is written on the board is written when he says « كنكتبو », not seconds before.
   - **The pointer (`highlight`) goes on the object named in the sentence, never on another one**: T1 when he says T1, the sphère pédonculée when he says sphère, NADH,H⁺ when he says NADH, the written equation when he reads it. The landmark is the tight box of what is drawn at that instant (a moving object: point at it when it is at rest); one landmark per object the voice names (`t1`…`t4` and `transporteurs`, `protons` of the space ≠ `protons_matrice`, the O₂ molecule `o2` ≠ the slider `poignee_o2`); a landmark whose object is not drawn is not declared, rather than falling back on a neighbour.
   - **A process described with no matching animation is a defect of the simulation, to be fixed in the simulation** (add the moment and its drawing), not hidden by moving the command or by a vague `highlight`.
   - **Each slide opens on its own state.** Two slides in a row with the same simulation URL keep the same iframe: the previous slide's finished scene stays and every `play_until` is refused (`sansRetourArriere`). A slide driven by the voice opens the page frozen where the previous one stopped (`?depart=<moment>`, distinct URL), with `concentration.demarrer: "reset"` (never `start`, which lets the scene run on its own, ahead of or behind the voice).
   - **Check it before saying it works**: `python backend/scripts/verifier_pointage_simulations.py` lists, for each sentence, the objects it names and the landmark aimed at, and flags a pointer on another object, a process said without its `play_until`, a missing moment, a reused URL, a free `start`, gestures too close (one per second); `node frontend/scripts/verifier-pointage-navigateur.mjs --sim <page>` replays the voice in Chromium with the player's timing, checks each moment is reached at its word and each pointer lands on a drawn landmark, and saves a capture of every pointer (`app/tests/test_pointage_simulations.py` keeps the SVT energy course at zero gap). The live tutor gets the same rule in `regles_du_pilotage` (`simulation_explication.py`).

40. **Make it easy to follow: what was transformed stays on the board, and the pointer waits for the object it names** (user rule, 17 November 2026 — on « La carte du chapitre » (`carrefour.html`): « il entoure le glucose alors qu'ici il est dégradé ; normalement il doit rester ici, marqué dégradé, pour bien comprendre et faciliter le suivi »). Complements rule 39:
   - **A reactant that is consumed or degraded does not vanish.** It stays at its place, faded and marked (« (dégradé) », « (oxydé) », « consommé »), and what it becomes appears beside it, linked by the arrow of the process, with the process's name written on that arrow (« glycolyse » under the glucose → pyruvic-acid arrow). Copies travel, the source stays: the student can retrace the whole story at any instant by looking at the board, and « le glucose » always has a glucose to point at. Same for a carrier that is unloaded (NADH,H⁺ → NAD⁺ stays visible), a molecule split, a substrate used.
   - **The pointer waits for its object and never points elsewhere.** When the landmark named is not drawn yet (the scene is still running towards its moment), `professor-locate.js` waits for it up to 3 s and `tableau.js` circles it as soon as it appears; meanwhile the professor does not fly to the middle of the simulation nor press a button in its place (`AIAvatar.tsx`). After 3 s with no landmark the command simply leaves — so a landmark that never appears at its word is a defect to fix (rule 39), not something the pointer hides.

41. **A national exercise is played, not just read: diagrams, animated curves and simulations, every phenomenon taken apart step by step** (user rule, 17 November 2026 — « rendre les nationales avec des schémas et des simulations, faire passer des courbes stables à des courbes animées, bien expliquer tous les phénomènes pas à pas, le décortiquer au maximum »). Every exercise taken from a national exam (`*_national_*` slides, a « National <année> <session> » séance, block exercises citing a subject):
    - **The set-up is drawn, then brought to life**: the exam's figure is redrawn on the board as an annotated diagram (source, medium, sensors, distances, the circuit with its arrows, the apparatus), then the same diagram is animated so the phenomenon is SEEN happening (the perturbation leaves S and reaches M, the pulse goes to the obstacle and comes back, the capacitor charges, the nuclei decay, the colour of the solution changes).
    - **A static curve becomes an animated curve**: an oscillogram, y(t), y(x), u_C(t), N(t), x(t), pH(V) is not shown finished. It is traced in time with the phenomenon (the spot of the oscilloscope moves, the curve grows while the animation runs), and the moment the question is about is frozen and marked (the start of each signal, τ between the two fronts, the tangent at the origin, 63 %, the equivalence point).
    - **Taken apart to the maximum, one step at a time** (rules 33 and 37): first the situation and what is measured, then each phenomenon of the exercise on its own moment (`play_until`), each reading on the curve with its scale (sensitivity, divisions × value), each relation with the line it comes from, then the calculation, then the answer. The question is asked at the moment it is needed, before its result is shown.
    - **Built with** the `simulations-au-tableau` skill (scene kit, moments, landmarks for the pointer, rules 1-13): reuse a view of the chapter when it already shows the phenomenon with the exam's values (pass the values as parameters), otherwise add a view to the chapter's simulation page. The exam's values are the simulation's values (same distances, sensitivities, durations), so what the student reads on the animated curve is what he reads in the exam.

Tables of reasoning (sign, variations, avancement), method tables and the bilan stay tables.

Before any explanation of a national exercise, the student sees the exercise as an exam sheet drawn in HTML: white paper, the official header of the exam (Royaume du Maroc, Ministère, Examen national du baccalauréat, session and year, subject, filière « Sciences physiques — option française », durée, coefficient, page n/N), the exercise title with its total points, every question numbered as in the exam with its points in the barème column on the right, the texts, data, figures, curves and tables redrawn cleanly from the original (`backend/data/exams/<matière>/<année>-<session>/exam.json` for the text and points, `pages/sujet_pN.jpg` and `assets/` only as the reference to copy from). One shared template for all subjects: `frontend/public/media/simulations/shared/examen/` (`feuille.css`, `feuille.js`, usage at the top of `feuille.js`). The slide's simulation is either `/media/simulations/shared/examen/feuille.html?sujet=<matière>/<année>-<session>&exercice=<n>[&questions=1-3][&page=3/6]` (text and points straight from `exam.json`, copied without the corrigé to `frontend/public/media/examens/` by `python scripts/examens/copier_sujets.py` — `--liste <sujet>` gives the exercise numbers and figures), or, when `exam.json` is not faithful or a figure must be redrawn, a page of its own `/media/simulations/shared/examen/sujets/<matière>-<année>-<session>-ex<n>.html` that sets `window.FEUILLE = { sujet, page, titre, blocs: [...] }` (model: `sujets/physique-2024-normale-ex2.html`, the 2024 normale water-wave exercise, its figure in SVG). While reading, the tutor highlights what it reads: `simulation_control` `surligner` `{id}` (or `highlight` `{target}`) with the ids `titre-exo`, `contexte`, `q-1`, `q-2.1`, `fig-…`, `tab-n`, `donnee-…` listed in the manifest; the manifest's `page_text` is the statement to read, barème included. The tutor reads the statement and its data as written; then the explanation starts, step by step, on the figure (rule 45). **The statement is complete and read aloud** (user rule, 21 November 2026, on SVT 2017 rattrapage ex. 2: « ces types d'examen doivent avoir un énoncé et il doit être lu »): the exercise's introduction (« Pour étudier… on propose les données suivantes »), the text that introduces each document, the « Remarque » and the « Données » are always on the sheet, before the document, and read by the tutor (with `contexte`, `intro`, `remarque` highlighted). They are copied word for word from the printed subject (`pages/sujet_pN.jpg`, or `extraction.json`, which keeps the subject as printed), never from `exam.json`'s `context` when it is a summary, and never a summary written by us (« Cet exercice porte sur… » is not in the subject: remove it). A sheet that starts directly at the document, or whose voice reads a summary, is a defect: fix the sheet and the `speech_text`, then regenerate the slide's voice. To audit all courses: compare each used exercise's `context` with `extraction.json`, and list `sujets/*.html` sheets without a `contexte` block.

47. **An association stays an association, with feedback on every pair** (user rule, 19 November 2026, on SVT ch1, national 2020 rattrapage, question III « Reliez chaque phénomène à la réaction bilan qui lui convient… », which had been split into three QCM: « pour les questions d'association, ne pas les transformer en QCM ; les mettre en association avec un feedback à chaque réponse, correcte ou fausse »). A national question « Reliez… / Associez… / Recopiez les couples (1 ; …) … et adressez à chaque numéro la lettre correspondante » is never cut into QCM: it is ONE slide question `type: 'association'` (`question.association`: `gauche`, `droite` with the subject's labels and its extra item if the subject has one, `couples`, `retours`, `bilan`). The student links the pairs himself — drag one item onto the other, or touch one then the other. **Every pair he forms gets its feedback at once, written and said**: right → confirmed, with one sentence saying why (« b donne l'acide lactique : c'est la fermentation lactique ») ; wrong → what is wrong about the item he chose, and a hint, never the right letter (« b donne l'acide lactique, ce n'est pas la glycolyse ; la glycolyse part du glucose et donne l'acide pyruvique : cherche la réaction qui fait ça »); after two errors on the same item the right pair is laid for him with its feedback. At the end, the line of couples exactly as the barème expects it, « (1 ; c) (2 ; e) (3 ; b) (4 ; a) », and the bilan (the extra item, the copy). Write one feedback per pair (`retours["1-c"]`, juste or not) or one default per left item (`retours["1-*"]`); their `dit` are new voice tracks (`<diapo>__q_assoc_<g>-<d>`, listed in `<deck>_voix.json`). Reader: `AssociationDiapo.tsx`; contract: `AssociationQuestion` in `types.ts`; audit: `docs/audit-association.md`.

48. **A national exercise is shown part by part: only the questions about to be answered** (user rule, 19 November 2026: « ne pas tout mettre dans une seule diapo ; mettre seulement la partie qui va être répondue ; afficher la partie que notre tuteur va traiter ; quelques questions répondues, puis la deuxième partie de l'exercice sur une nouvelle diapo, et on répond avec le tuteur »). Never a statement slide with the whole exercise (4 to 10 questions, 2 to 4 pages). The exercise is cut into **parts of 1 to 3 questions that are answered together** — consecutive questions on the same object or the same document (« 1 et 2 : la célérité », « 3 et 4 : la diffraction »), or, in a course mixing subjects, a few questions picked from different exams on the same notion. Each part is: **one statement slide** (rule 46: the exam sheet, read by the tutor) showing the context, the data, the documents those questions use and **only those questions** — `feuille.html?sujet=…&exercice=<n>&questions=<positions>` (positions in the exercise's question list, « 1-2 », « 3,4 »), or `&partie=<k>` on a page of its own whose `blocs` carry `partie: k` (a bloc with no `partie` — the context, a shared document — shows in every part) — **then the slides that answer them** (demonstration on the figure, rule 45; student question). Then the next part, on a new slide, the same way. Part 1 keeps the full official header and the exercise title with its total points; parts 2… keep the short header and the title. The tutor reads the context in full in part 1 only; from part 2 on, one sentence recalls it (« même exercice : l'onde à la surface de l'eau ») and he reads the new data and questions. Each part's statement must fit on ONE page of the sheet with no « page suivante » (check `Feuille.etat().pages === 1` at 1200×650); if not, the part is too big — cut it again. Each statement slide's `speech_text` reads only its part: new voice tracks (`<diapo>` ids `<orig>_p2`, `<orig>_p3`…, listed in `docs/parties_voix/<deck>.json`). Made precise the same day: **a part never shows a figure or text of another part** (« pas la peine de mettre la figure du périodique alors qu'on est dans la radioactivité ») — in `exam.json`, part texts and part data go in `ex.intros` with `intro_cles` on their questions, each figure is cited by the questions that use it (`q.documents`; with `questions=` an uncited document is hidden unless `commun: true`), `place: "droite"` as on the subject; **`exam.json` is first checked against the scan** `pages/sujet_pN.jpg` (21 of 21 exercises checked had errors: merged parts, missing question, wrong points or figure) and fixed, then `copier_sujets.py`; **the text stays large** (zoom ≥ 0.75, short header on page 1 when needed); **the statement comes before any slide that uses its exercise's data** (a course slide that only quotes a national value keeps it as an example without naming the national). SVT keeps its own statement pages (user's choice).

49. **Every course opens with a welcome and closes with advice, said by the teacher** (user rule, 19 November 2026 — « ajouter une ouverture orale de bienvenue et du nom du cours, avec une fermeture du cours avec quelques conseils généraux, à chaque terminaison de cours »). Like a teacher entering and leaving the class, always in the same form so the student recognises the ritual: **at the start**, he welcomes the student, says the name of the course as the programme names it, what will be learnt and why it matters at the bac (the weight of its domain at the national), and how we work (listen, answer the questions at the board, write in the notebook when the course stops) — 20 to 35 s, warm and sober. **At the end of the course**, he says what was learnt (three key ideas at most, formulas in French words), general advice to revise and for exam day (reread the notebook, redo the national exercises alone, write every step as the marking scheme reads it, share your time and start with the easiest question, rest the night before), an encouragement, and the next course of the subject when it exists — 30 to 50 s; the advice also goes into the notebook. Slides, data and voice: skill `cours-concentration`, step 9d.

50. **The answer of a national question says its number, in the demonstration and in the notebook, on the subject's own diagram, commented** (user rule, 21 November 2026, on radioactivity, national 2024 rattrapage ex. 3: « dans la correction, il doit ajouter dans la démo le numéro de la question à répondre qui se trouve dans l'examen, et aussi dans le cahier à écrire ; et ajouter le schéma du national avec des commentaires pour être bien suivi par l'élève »). The student must always know which exam question he is answering, and see the diagram he will have on the exam day:
   - **The question number, as printed in the subject** (« 1-1 », « 2.3 », « II.2 », « Partie 2 – 1 »): it opens the answer slide's title (« National 2024 R, exercice 3 : questions 1-1 et 1-2 »), its voice (« السؤال un point un… », « Question un point un : »), each step of the board demonstration that answers it (the step's text starts with « 1-1 : »), the slide's `concentration.cahier` and **the notebook lines of the block** (`seances[].blocs[].cahier`: « 1-1 : flèche (1) … → β⁻ » ; « 1-2 : … X = ²⁰⁸₈₂Pb »). A numbered notebook part (« 12. National 2024 R, exercice 3 — … : ») groups the exercise's answers; its question numbers are written « 1-1 : » (never « 1. », which would open a new part).
   - **The subject's diagram, not ours.** The animated figure of a national answer is the exam's figure redrawn exactly: the same nuclides, points, curves, axes (which quantity on which axis), the same labels and **the same numbering** ((1), (2), (3), A, B…) — nothing added that is not in the subject, nothing renumbered. **Compare it with the scan** (`backend/data/exams/<matière>/<année>-<session>/pages/sujet_pN.jpg`, `assets/`) and with the official correction (`pages/correction_pN.jpg`) before writing the answer: on 21 November 2026 the 2024 R view had its own arrows « (1) α, (2) β⁻ » towards a ²¹²Po absent from the subject, and the course taught « flèche un : alpha » where the correction says 1-1 = β⁻.
   - **Commented**: each element the answer uses lights up in turn with a short comment beside it, starting with the question number when it answers one (« 1-1 : (1) A = 212, Z + 1 → β⁻ », « 1-2 : X = ²⁰⁸₈₂Pb »); the other elements of the figure stay visible, pale, as on the sheet.
   - **The same diagram goes into the notebook**, smaller, with the same numbers and comments (`cahier_figures`, `type: "schema"`, `partie` = the national's notebook part; elements `point`, `fleche` with « (1) », `texte` for the comments), so the student copies the figure he will meet on the exam.
   - The voice is regenerated (the slide's text changes: remove `audio`, list the track for the batch). Model: `phys_ch4_radioactivite`, slide `ra_f3_famille2024`, block `ra_s3_b2`, view `segre.html?vue=famille2024`.

## How it is implemented

- `backend/app/services/skill_professeur.py` — `SkillProfesseur` keeps three facts per session (a question is pending, number of attempts, turns) and returns the turn's instruction block `[GESTE DE PROF — ce tour]`, injected into the system prompt before the model speaks. The model declares its judgement of the student's answer at the start of its reply in `<eval>correct|partiel|faux|incertain|aucune</eval>`; the module reads it to count attempts (`incertain`: the answer cannot be judged, so the tutor asks once for clarification without counting an attempt), and `tag_decoder` strips the tag from chat, voice and history.
- Lesson phases (`lesson_phase.py`, `PHASE_RULES` in `llm_service.py`) say what the phase is for; the skill says what to do in this turn.
- Surfaces (scene, image, 3D, simulation, notebook) are chosen by `routeur_pedagogique.py`; explanation tactics per student by `teaching_tactics.py`; tone by `humanisation.py`.
- The animated professor (`frontend/src/services/professorEmotion.js`, `AIAvatar.tsx`, `Professor3D.tsx`) reacts to the tutor's words: greets, celebrates earned success, puts hands on heart, nods to encourage, laughs, is surprised; it points, writes on the board and operates simulations itself. The declared `<eval>` also reaches it directly (`professor_reaction`: correct → arms up, faux → shakes his head, partiel → nod), as does a right or wrong check inside a lab.
- Simulations drawn on the board (`docs/design/labos-au-tableau.md`) announce pointable landmarks and an explanation script; `backend/app/services/simulation_explication.py` puts them in the prompt (`bloc_prompt`): the tutor explains landmark by landmark with `highlight {target}` (the professor flies into the simulation and points), relaunches the simulation itself when the student does not understand, and after a failed check the server gives one hint pointing at the missing landmark, then relaunches and re-explains differently (`decider_reaction`, `_sim_reagir_verification`).

## When changing the teaching behaviour

- Change the rule in `skill_professeur.py` and its test `app/tests/test_skill_professeur.py` together; keep instructions imperative and short — a long instruction gets paraphrased instead of followed.
- Never make the model's judgement implicit: if a new state depends on what the student did, it must be declared in the reply (a tag) or measured from the conversation, never guessed.
- Run `python -m pytest app/tests/test_skill_professeur.py app/tests/test_experience_profs.py app/tests/test_tag_decoder.py app/tests/test_lesson_phase.py app/tests/test_simulation_explication.py app/tests/test_pointage_simulations.py` from `backend`.
