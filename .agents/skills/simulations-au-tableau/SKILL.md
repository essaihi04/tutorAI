# Simulations au tableau

A course simulation is a small HTML page drawn with chalk ON the course board. It is not a separate app: the student sees the board, the chalk writing, and one control. The tutor reads what it shows in real time and drives it like a teacher at the blackboard. Reference set: `frontend/public/media/simulations/physics/ondes-tableau/` (corde, micros, sonar — waves example course, November 2026). Kit and protocol details: `docs/design/labos-au-tableau.md`.

## Rules from the user

1. **Minimum buttons.** One main button that cycles (Lancer → Pause → Reprendre → Rejouer). Every setting is a handle you drag on the drawing (`Tableau.glisser`): the point M on the rope, the second microphone, the sea floor. No slider bars, no tabs unless a variant really changes the scene.
2. **Transparent background that blends into the board.** The page draws on the board the course player already shows: `html, body { background: transparent !important }`, no grid image, and `:root { color-scheme: normal }` — if the iframe's color-scheme differs from the course page, Chrome paints an opaque background behind it. `MediaViewer` gives `/media/simulations/` pages a transparent surface with no padding or coloured frame (a light-blue frame once made the chalk invisible).
3. **Everything visible.** The whole drawing fits a fixed virtual frame (760 × 420, scaled with `cadre()`/`local()` in the scene helper): nothing cut, no scrolling, labels never overlapping (check every label at the extreme handle positions).
4. **Controls at the TOP, bottom strip free.** The course player lays its own bar over the bottom of the board (pause, « Poser une question », ↺, « Suivant → », the slide counter). A button or bubble at the bottom of the simulation is hidden under it (7 November 2026: « Rejouer » was under « ↺ » and « Suivant »). Put the `.barre` first (`.barre { order: -1 }`) and keep about 4.5em empty at the bottom of the scene (`.scene { padding-bottom: 4.5em }`).
5. **Only the programme.** Values, formulas and words come from the chapter's `backend/data/curriculum/perimetre.json`; nothing out of programme on the drawing (no √(T/μ) for the waves chapter, no superposition…). The board text is in French, the BAC BIOF language.
6. **Drawn live, like a teacher.** Use the `Craie` kit (`shared/tableau/tableau.{css,js}`): fixed parts written stroke by stroke, the moving part drawn with `craie.direct`, the rest appearing when it happens. Render on demand (only while something moves or chalk is still writing).
7. **Real media look real** (user rule, 9 November 2026: « utiliser un fond comme de l'eau, rendre les ondes plus réalistes »). Chalk is for the teacher's writing (labels, arrows, dimensions, axes); the physical medium itself is painted, inside its frame only, with the raw canvas (`craie.ctx` inside `craie.direct`):
   - **Water seen from above**: a basin gradient clipped to the view's frame, faint slow sky reflections (alpha ≈ 0.035), ripples drawn as a radial gradient whose ring colour follows the SLOPE of the surface (lit side light, other side dark), a thin light line on the crest, a slight visual fade with distance; a splash when the drop lands; the drop itself a filled droplet.
   - **Water seen from the side**: a filled slice under the surface curve (lighter at the top, darker in depth) with a bright surface line; a floating object is drawn first and the water over it, so it sits half-submerged.
   - **Objects**: shaded spheres (radial gradient, highlight, soft shadow), not chalk circles; an object rising in a top view can grow slightly.
   - The painted look never changes the physics: the model (`y = y_S(t − x/v)`), the key instants, `ce_qui_se_voit` and the measurements stay exact; a fade or a reflection is for the eye only and is said so in a comment. Draw the medium BEFORE the chalk frame and labels so the chalk stays on top and readable. Reference: `ondes-tableau/ballon.html` (`eauDessus`, `eauCote`, `balle`, `goutte`).
   - Apply it to every medium a student knows from life: water, a rope (thickness, shading), air/sound (soft pressure bands), a sea floor (sand, depth gradient), light. Keep it light: one gradient per frame, no heavy per-pixel loops (render on demand still applies).
8. **Physics that reads well.** Choose shapes that make the point visible: the waves pulse is asymmetric (fast rise, slow fall), otherwise the mirror between the rope's shape and the elongation y(t) cannot be seen. Values chosen so the numbers are round (v = 1 m/s, θ = 0,4 s; v = 340 m/s with a 0,17 m step = 0,5 ms = one division; v = 1500 m/s with a 7,5 m step = 10 ms).
9. **Fill the board, nothing tiny, nothing on top of something else** (user rule, 16 November 2026, on two captures of the glycolysis slide: « il y a plus d'espace mais il reste vide et l'écriture est petite », « certains schémas débordent », « la mitochondrie : ne pas reproduire »). The course player often gives the simulation a frame only about 1000 to 1350 px wide and 500 px high, with the tutor's character over the right edge.
   - **Use the space.** The main figure takes most of the frame's height; side blocks (counter, legend, equation) are placed next to it and enlarged, never a small figure in a corner with empty board around it. If more than about a third of the frame stays empty at the end of the animation, enlarge the figure and the writing.
   - **Writing large.** 15 px on screen is the floor, not the target: titles, counters and the numbers the lesson is about are around 20 to 26 px on screen when there is room.
   - **No overlap, at every instant.** A label never sits on a painted object, on another label or across the edge of a cell; put it beside the object with a short reminder line. Check at the start, the middle and the end of the animation, not only on the final picture.
   - **Nothing too small to recognise.** An organelle or object that is drawn must show its structure: a mitochondrion shows its two membranes and its cristae (at least about 90 px long on screen), in bright colours, with its name beside it. If there is no room, enlarge the cell or move the other objects; do not shrink the organelle.
   - **Do not repeat the slide.** The slide already shows its title: no internal title that says the same thing.
   - **Test narrow.** Check at 1024 × 560 as well as 1280 and 1596 wide.
10. **Writing is always clearly visible** (user rule, 16 November 2026, on four captures: white text on light-blue NAD⁺ / NADH,H⁺ capsules, white T1–T4 on violet proteins, a pale equation not centred in the blue band of the Krebs cycle, mitochondria spilling out of the bioreactor and of the hyaloplasm strip).
   - **Contrast first.** Text written ON a painted object (capsule, protein, card, band) is dark and bold on a light object (`#0b1f2a` on cyan/orange/yellow, `#1a0838` on violet) and white only on a dark object. Chalk text on the board uses the bright chalk colours (`jaune`, `cyan`, `vert`, `orange`, `rose`); `faible` / `tresfaible` only for secondary notes, never for an equation, a result or a name the lesson is about.
   - **Text fits its shape.** A label inside a capsule fits inside it with a margin (enlarge the capsule rather than shrink the text); neighbouring capsules do not touch.
   - **Centred in its band.** A line written in a coloured band or frame is centred in it and does not overflow it: if it is too long, split it into two lines.
   - **Objects stay in their container.** Every organelle, cell or particle painted in a container (beaker, bioreactor, cell, hyaloplasm strip) stays inside it with a margin, clear of the probes and the walls — compute the positions from the container's inner edges, rotation included.

11. **Chemical equations on the board carry the physical state of each species** (user rule, 16 November 2026): CH₃COOH(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ CH₃COO⁻(aq) + H₃O⁺(aq), Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s), Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂(g), esterification all (l); the state is written small and low, like an index. In the chemistry folders it is automatic: `window.Etats` (end of each `commun.js`, and `chimie/shared/chem-lab.js`) annotates every equation drawn with `c.texte` and the page helpers `eqn`, `eqf`, `eqn2`, `ligneM`, `placer`, `morceau`, `termes` (their first line calls `Etats.cotes` / `Etats.morceaux` / `Etats.liste`), keeps the writing time of the equation without states (moments unchanged) and shrinks a line that would leave the frame. A new helper that draws an equation in pieces calls `window.Etats` the same way. Only the board: the voice, the bubble, `voir()` and `explication` keep plain equations.

12. **Relations are written like in the notebook: a division is a real fraction, never a bar** (user rule, 17 November 2026, on the waves course and its « Ce qu'il faut retenir » — « l'écriture doit être bien écrite comme dans le cahier pour les relations » — for the simulations, the bilan fiches and the chapter map). On the board, `v = d / Δt`, `τ = SM / v`, `d = v · τ / 2` are never written flat: the kit draws the numerator above a chalk line and the denominator below with the notation `⟦num|dén⟧` in any `craie.texte` string — `'v = ⟦d|Δt⟧'`, `'τ = ⟦SM|v⟧'`, `'d = ⟦v·τ|2⟧'`, `'L = ⟦2λD|a⟧'`, `'λ = ⟦ln2|t½⟧'`, values `⟦${fr(a)}|${fr(b)}⟧`. Subscripts (`y_M`, `u_C`) work inside.
   - **Measure with the kit**: `craie.largeurTexte(s, taille)` knows the width of a fraction; never `ctx.measureText` on a string that contains `⟦`.
   - **Make room**: a fraction is about 1,9 times as tall as a line. Every page that writes one gives it the height (interligne, taller formula frame) and is checked for overlaps; in a fiche with fixed-height blocks the block stays closed (model: `physics/ondes-tableau/bilan.html`, `bloc()` and `etages`).
   - **Text that is not chalk** (bubble, `explication`, `vu`, `motif`, aria-label, tutor text): no `⟦⟧`; pass a chalk string through `window.Tableau.lisible(s)` (« d/Δt »), and write a division without spaces around the bar, with parentheses around a numerator or denominator of several terms (« (v·τ)/2 », « (12 V)/(12 mA) »).
   - **The bar stays a bar** only where it is not a division: units (m/s, mol/L), couples (AH/A⁻), t₁/₂, inside an exponent (e^(−t/τ)), between two words or options (et/ou, sain/malade, 5'/3').
   - Greek letters are written as symbols on the board and in every written text (λ, τ, θ, Δm), never « lambda », « tau », « delta m » — those words belong to the voice only.
   - **Check**: `python -I scripts/verifier_formules.py` lists the flat divisions left in the chalk strings of the simulations and in the written text of the courses; it must print nothing for the folder you touched.

13. **What was transformed stays visible, marked** (user rule, 17 November 2026, on `carrefour.html` — the glucose slid away to become the pyruvic acids and the pointer then circled an empty spot; professeur rule 40). A reactant consumed or degraded stays at its place, faded and marked (« (dégradé) », « (oxydé) »); copies travel to become the products, linked by the process arrow carrying the process's name. Its landmark stays declared (on the faded source) as long as it is drawn.

14. **National exercises: the exam's figure comes alive** (user rule, 17 November 2026; professeur rule 41). For a slide built on a national subject: redraw the exam's diagram (annotated) and animate it with the exam's own values; never show a finished curve — trace it in time with the phenomenon (oscilloscope spot, growing curve), then freeze on each reading the question needs (fronts, τ, divisions × sensitivity, tangent, 63 %, equivalence) with a landmark per reading so the pointer can show it. One moment per step of the reasoning, results of later steps hidden until their moment.

## The tutor must be able to do everything

The tutor explains with the simulation, answers a free question with it, relaunches it as often as needed, pauses on the right instant, moves an element, points at it, or brings another simulation of the lesson. So each simulation:

- **Declares itself** with `Tableau.protocole({ id, commandes, config, texte, etat, progression, executer })`: manifest (`simulation_manifest`), state (`simulation_state`) and commands (`simulation_control`, fields `command` and **`parameters`**).
- **Accepts the commands it can honour** and lists them with a schema: `start`, `reset`, `pause`, `resume`, `fire_pulse` (one-shot experiments), `set_time {time_s}` (freeze on an instant), `set_parameters {…}` (with the range and step, e.g. `x_m: '0.3..1.9'`). `highlight {target}` is added by the kit.
- **Exposes its key instants, computed from the physics** — `moments` in the state (corde: `source_monte`, `m_atteint`, `m_monte`, `m_sommet`, `m_descend`, `m_revenu`, `toute_sur_corde`, `fin`; recomputed when M is moved; micros: `son_part`, `m1_atteint`, `m2_atteint`, `signal_m1`, `signal_m2`, `mesure`; sonar: `salve_descend`, `fond_atteint`, `salve_remonte`, `echo_recu`) — and accepts **`play_until {moment | time_s}`**: replays (from zero if the instant is past, else continues) and freezes on that instant with « Arrêt sur image : … » in the bubble. A frozen scene must still draw what is moving (front, salve, spot): draw it when `running || pause`, not only while running. This is what lets the tutor show each phase while he says it (rule 18 of the `professeur` skill). Every new simulation lists, before anything else, the instants a teacher would freeze on, and gives them a name.
- **Says who acted.** The kit (`Tableau.protocole`) records `par: 'tuteur' | 'eleve'` on every action and `derniere_main` in the state: a command received → the tutor; a pointer or key inside the page → the student; `personne` until then. The player reacts only to what the student did himself.
- **Variants are shown, then left to the student** (user rule, 8 November 2026). When a simulation has something to vary (M₂ moved along the ruler, M moved along the rope, the sea bottom raised or lowered), the slide declares `concentration.variante` (`apres_s`, `etapes`: `set_parameters`, `play_until`, `highlight`). Five seconds after the voice, if the student has not touched the scene, the tutor does it himself — new setting, replay, point at what changed — so the comparison is seen. If the student does it himself and gets a new result (state `finished`, `derniere_main: 'eleve'`, different `parameters`), nothing opens by itself: a « 🔎 Ton résultat » button blinks at the bottom of the board (user rule, 10 November 2026) and the course goes on; only when the student presses it does the player pause the course, send before/after to `POST /course-player/sim-reaction` (DeepSeek: interpretation + one question), says everything written in the panel with the live voice by pieces (the text appears at the rhythm of the voice, `parole_question` reads the question and its choices), then the question (QCM, vrai/faux or direct, chosen from the student's previous answers on that slide: `simulation_reaction.type_de_question`), each option with its own spoken feedback (`retours`, falling back on `parole_juste` / `parole_faux`; a direct answer is judged by the LLM); once the feedback is said the panel closes by itself and the course resumes on the same slide.
- **Names its views.** A scene with several views labels each one on the chalk (« Vue de dessus », « Vue de côté ») and declares a `repere` on each, so the tutor can point at a view while he says what it shows (professeur rule 21).
- **Says what is on screen, in words** — `ce_qui_se_voit` in the state: the time, where the front is, what each point is doing, which curve is drawn, the value displayed. It is what the tutor « sees » (no OCR needed: the page knows exactly what it drew). Emit the state on every event and every 500 ms while it runs.
- **Announces pointable landmarks** (`craie.repere(id, …)`) and an explanation script per variant (`Tableau.explication({ variante: [{ repere, texte }] })`); `backend/app/services/simulation_explication.py` puts them in the prompt with the piloting rules.
- Each control keeps `data-professor-control="cmd …"` and the page loads `/media/simulations/professor-locate.js`, so the animated professor flies to the button and presses it.
- To let the tutor open **another** simulation, the simulations of the lesson must be registered as lesson resources in the database (only after the user validates the course).

## Traps already met

- **Apostrophes inside JavaScript strings.** French text in a single-quoted string (`'la source n'a pas…'`) breaks the whole script: the page then shows only its button. Write the typographic apostrophe `’` in every French string, or use backticks.
- **Declare before use.** The scene object must exist before the chalk loop starts (`Cannot access 'scene' before initialization`): create it, then start with `requestAnimationFrame`.
- **The in-app browser pane blocks iframes inside the course player** (`ERR_BLOCKED_BY_CLIENT`); test the page directly or inside a same-origin test page.

## Check before saying it works

1. Syntax: extract each `<script>` block and run `node --check` on it.
2. In the browser, for every variant URL: no console error, the canvas has ink, the button is there.
3. Drive it like the tutor, from a test page that loads it in an iframe and posts `{type: 'simulation_control', command, parameters}`: pause, set a parameter, set a time, resume, fire again. Read back `current_state`: status, parameters and `ce_qui_se_voit` must match, and the numbers must be physically right (e.g. M at 1,2 m with v = 1 m/s → τ = 1,20 s; d = 1,02 m → τ = 3 ms = 6 divisions; floor at 60 m → echo after 80 ms).
4. Open the slide in the course player (`/dev/course-player?scene=…&diapo=<slide id>`) and look at the bottom: nothing of the simulation under the player's bar.
5. An overview page of the whole course (board text, simulations live, questions, voice) helps the user review: `physics/ondes-tableau/apercu-cours.html` is generated from the course JSON.
6. Capture each view at 1024 × 560, 1280 × 640 and 1596 × 700, at the start, the middle and the end: no label over an object or another label, nothing cut, no large empty area, no writing under 15 px on screen, every organelle recognisable (rule 9); every text readable on what is under it, centred in its band, every object inside its container (rule 10).
